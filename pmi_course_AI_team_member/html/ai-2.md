### 5 AI Ethical Principles

**established by the USA DoD in 2020**  
[https://www.defense.gov/Watch/Video/videoid/740566](https://www.defense.gov/Watch/Video/videoid/740566)

* **Responsible:** Humans (DOD personnel) are responsible for the development, deployment, and use of AI
* **Equitable:** Unintended bias in AI capabilities must be minimized
* **Traceable:** Humans (relevant personnel) will possess an appropriate understanding of the technology, development processes, and operational methods applicable to AI. AI must have auditable methodologies, data sources, and design procedures and documentation
* **Reliable:** AI must have well-defined uses. The safety, security, and effectiveness of AI will be subject to testing across their entire life cycle
* **Governable:** AI will possess the ability to detect and avoid unintended consequences. Humans will have the tools to deactivate AI that demonstrates unintended behavior

### Do traditional risk responses apply to AI?

#### Cybersecurity Laws for Internet of Things
[https://www.cnn.com/2018/11/09/opinions/cybersecurity-laws-internet-of-things-schneier/index.html](https://www.cnn.com/2018/11/09/opinions/cybersecurity-laws-internet-of-things-schneier/index.html)

California's SB327 law took effect January 2020. Key concepts include:
* **"connected devices":** just about anything connected to the internet
* **"reasonable security":** undefined
* **default passwords are not allowed:** GOOD

Security features must protect the device and info from a variety of threats. California's attorney general will interpret. California SB327 extends beyond borders to anyone selling in California much like European General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).
